## Write: explain transactions and why they matter, without googling

Actually write this — a few paragraphs, no reference material, as if explaining to someone who's never heard of ACID. Then self-check against this rubric rather than a model answer, since the value here is in *your* explanation, not a copy of mine:

- Did you explain **why** atomicity matters using a concrete example (your own `create_borrow()`, not an abstract "bank transfer" you memorized from a textbook)?
- Did you distinguish what happens **without** a transaction — specifically, what partial state becomes possible — not just assert that transactions "prevent problems"?
- Did you mention isolation as a *separate* concern from atomicity? A lot of people conflate the two; they solve different problems (atomicity: does this operation happen completely or not at all; isolation: can concurrent operations see each other's half-finished work).
- Could you explain `READ COMMITTED` vs `SERIALIZABLE` in one sentence each, with the last-copy race condition from Day 7 as the motivating example?

If your explanation could be given by someone who'd only read the words "ACID" and "BEGIN/COMMIT/ROLLBACK" without ever running the Day 7 practice exercise, it's not grounded enough yet — go back and re-run that exercise, then rewrite the explanation.

## Write: explain the aggregation pipeline to a junior dev

Same rule — actually write it, no notes. Self-check:

- Did you lead with the **stage-by-stage data flow** mental model (Day 21's diagram) rather than jumping straight to `$match`/`$group` syntax? A junior dev needs the shape of the idea before the specific operators.

- Did you give at least one concrete example pipeline, not just abstract stage names?
- Would a junior dev, after reading your explanation, know *why* `$match` should generally go as early as possible — tying back to Day 6's indexing lesson (an early `$match` can use an index; a late one can't)?
